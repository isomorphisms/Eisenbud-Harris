# Fulton ↔ Eisenbud–Harris: intersection theory bridge

Canonical Fulton home: https://github.com/walnut-burgundy/fulton

## Why the repositories should cross-link

Eisenbud's *Commutative Algebra* supplies much of the algebraic machinery that later appears geometrically: localization, associated primes, dimension and codimension, flatness, differentials, homological methods, free resolutions, and constructive Gröbner-basis methods.

*The Geometry of Schemes* packages that algebra into scheme language.

*3264 and All That* then uses concrete enumerative problems to develop Chow rings, Grassmannians, Chern classes, projective bundles, Segre classes, degeneracy loci/Porteous' formula, excess intersections, blow-ups, and Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch.

Fulton's *Intersection Theory* is the systematic reference on the intersection-theoretic side. Its second edition develops rational equivalence, divisors, vector bundles and Chern classes, intersection products, and the broader formalism in which many of the computations in *3264 and All That* live.

## Reading relation

Do not flatten these books into duplicates:

- **Eisenbud, *Commutative Algebra***: algebraic mechanisms and their geometric meaning.
- **Eisenbud–Harris, *The Geometry of Schemes***: scheme language and geometric constructions.
- **Eisenbud–Harris, *3264 and All That***: concrete enumerative problems and working intersection-theory technique.
- **Fulton, *Intersection Theory***: systematic intersection-theory framework and reference.

## Topics to cross-index

- dimension and codimension;
- regular embeddings and normal cones/bundles;
- rational equivalence and Chow groups/rings;
- Chern and Segre classes;
- projective bundles;
- degeneracy loci and Porteous-type formulas;
- excess intersection;
- blow-ups;
- Riemann–Roch;
- computational examples where Macaulay2 can expose the underlying ideal/module structure.
