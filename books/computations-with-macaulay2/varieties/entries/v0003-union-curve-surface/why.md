# Why this variety appears

The example forces a distinction that is easy to reverse when moving between equations and geometry.

For algebraic sets, intersecting the defining ideals corresponds to taking the **union** of the zero sets. In this example Macaulay2 also verifies that the product of the two ideals equals that ideal intersection.

This is exactly why the catalog must not “make an intersection” by multiplying defining polynomials. For two hypersurfaces, `f × g = 0` describes their union, not their simultaneous intersection.
