# Why this polynomial appears

The opening computation starts with two equations in the affine ring `QQ[x,y,z]`. This is the first generator.

On its own, `x⁴ − y⁵ = 0` cuts out a hypersurface in affine three-space; the variable `z` remains free. The book immediately pairs it with `x³ − y⁷` so that the ideal carries more structure than either equation alone.

Keeping this polynomial separate matters because later operations act on the ideal generated by both equations, while SURFER can still render the individual hypersurface. The pair also gives a concrete place to distinguish “one equation”, “a system of equations”, and the geometry produced by the system.

Next questions worth preserving: what components does the two-generator ideal have, what does saturation remove, and how does the geometry change after replacing the original ideal by its saturated component?
