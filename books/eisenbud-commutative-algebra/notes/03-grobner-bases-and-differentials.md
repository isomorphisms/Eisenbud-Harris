# Gröbner bases and modules of differentials

Gröbner bases and Kähler differentials sit next to each other in the book, but they answer almost opposite questions.

- Gröbner theory makes polynomial ideals discrete and algorithmic by selecting leading terms.
- Differentials discard higher-order terms and expose first-order/infinitesimal geometry.

Used together, they provide a practical bridge from equations to geometry.

## Part I — Gröbner bases

### 1. Term orders and initial ideals

A monomial order is a total order on monomials compatible with multiplication and with `1` minimal. Given such an order, every nonzero polynomial has a leading monomial/term.

For an ideal `I`, its initial ideal `in(I)` is generated by leading terms of all elements of `I`.

The crucial move is replacing a complicated ideal by a monomial ideal while retaining significant graded information.

Standard monomials—monomials not in `in(I)`—form a vector-space basis for the quotient in the usual polynomial-ring setting. Consequently `I` and `in(I)` have the same Hilbert function in graded/homogeneous contexts.

### 2. Gröbner bases

A finite set `G = {g_1,…,g_r}` is a Gröbner basis of `I` when

`in(I) = (in(g_1),…,in(g_r))`.

Equivalently, multivariate division by `G` provides a canonical-enough remainder for ideal-membership purposes.

Applications:

- decide whether `f ∈ I`;
- compute normal forms in `A/I`;
- compute elimination ideals;
- solve zero-dimensional systems by reducing to triangular structure;
- compute Hilbert functions via monomial ideals;
- feed syzygy and resolution algorithms.

### 3. Buchberger's criterion

Leading terms can cancel, so a generating set need not be a Gröbner basis. `S`-polynomials target precisely these possible cancellations.

Buchberger's algorithm repeatedly forms and reduces `S`-polynomials until every relevant critical pair reduces to zero. Termination follows from Noetherianity of monomial ideals / Dickson-type finiteness.

The conceptual point matters more than memorizing the algorithm: ideal membership becomes finite because the leading-term ideal eventually stabilizes.

### 4. Elimination

Choose an order that places variables to eliminate above the variables to keep. Then a Gröbner basis for `I` contains enough elements in the smaller polynomial ring to generate the elimination ideal.

Example:

`I = (xy-1, y²-x) ⊂ k[x,y]`.

From `x=y²` and `xy=1`, obtain `y³=1`; similarly `x³=1`. An elimination computation makes these consequences algorithmic without informal substitution.

### 5. Degeneration

Passing from `I` to `in(I)` can be understood geometrically as a degeneration to a combinatorial object. Monomial ideals encode unions/thickenings of coordinate subspaces and are much easier to inspect.

This idea points forward to:

- flat degenerations;
- Hilbert schemes;
- toric methods;
- combinatorial commutative algebra;
- Gröbner methods for syzygies.

Whenever the notes use an initial ideal, record which invariants survive the degeneration and which do not. Equality of Hilbert function does not imply equality of singularities, components, or reducedness.

## Part II — Kähler differentials

### 6. Universal derivations

For a ring map `R → A`, the module of Kähler differentials `Ω_{A/R}` comes with a universal `R`-derivation

`d : A → Ω_{A/R}`

such that every `R`-derivation `A → M` factors uniquely through an `A`-linear map `Ω_{A/R} → M`.

This universal property is the definition worth remembering. Symbolic rules

`d(a+b)=da+db`, `d(ab)=a db+b da`, and `dr=0` for `r∈R`

are consequences encoded by the construction.

For a polynomial ring

`A = R[x_1,…,x_n]`,

`Ω_{A/R}` is free with basis `dx_1,…,dx_n`.

### 7. Quotients and the conormal sequence

If `A = P/I` with `P` an `R`-algebra, then the basic exact sequence is

`I/I² → Ω_{P/R} ⊗_P A → Ω_{A/R} → 0`.

The first map sends the class of `f` to `df`.

For `P = k[x_1,…,x_n]`, this presents `Ω_{A/k}` by the Jacobian matrix of generators of `I`.

This sequence is one of the central bridges between equations and first-order geometry.

### 8. Tangent and cotangent spaces

For a local `k`-algebra `(A,m)` with residue field `k` at a rational point, the cotangent space is

`m/m²`

and the tangent space is its dual

`Hom_k(m/m²,k)`.

Equivalently, tangent vectors can be described as `k`-derivations `A → k`.

For an affine variety defined by equations `f_1,…,f_r`, the tangent space at a point is the kernel of the evaluated Jacobian matrix.

### 9. Singularity detection

For a variety of local dimension `d`, a smooth point should have tangent-space dimension `d` under the standard finite-type/perfect-field hypotheses appropriate to the Jacobian criterion.

Example: the cusp

`A = k[x,y]/(y²-x³)`.

The Jacobian row is

`(-3x², 2y)`.

At `(0,0)` it vanishes (assuming characteristics that do not collapse the coefficients in a misleading way), so the tangent space has dimension two although the curve has dimension one. The origin is singular.

Characteristic matters. A Jacobian test must never silently assume characteristic zero.

### 10. Differentials and maps

For composable ring maps `R → A → B`, one has an exact sequence

`Ω_{A/R} ⊗_A B → Ω_{B/R} → Ω_{B/A} → 0`.

This separates infinitesimal motion inherited from the base from relative motion in the fibers.

The relative differential module `Ω_{B/A}` therefore belongs naturally beside flatness and dimension of fibers when studying morphisms.

## Combined computational workflow

For an affine algebra `A = k[x_1,…,x_n]/I`:

1. compute a Gröbner basis of `I`;
2. inspect dimension/Hilbert data via `in(I)`;
3. compute associated/minimal primes when useful;
4. form the Jacobian matrix;
5. compute the presentation of `Ω_{A/k}`;
6. locate rank drops / singular loci;
7. compute a free resolution if the module or ideal warrants it.

This workflow should become the default Macaulay2 bridge for the book.

## Theorem/computation ledger

- existence of Gröbner bases;
- Buchberger criterion;
- equality of Hilbert functions for an ideal and its initial ideal in the graded setting;
- elimination theorem;
- universal property of `Ω`;
- conormal exact sequence;
- transitivity exact sequence for differentials;
- tangent-space identification with dual of `m/m²`;
- Jacobian criterion with field and finiteness hypotheses written explicitly.
